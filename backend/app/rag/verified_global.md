# ShunShi international edition: verified baseline health information

## Sleep

### Adult sleep duration and sleep habits

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) states that adults aged 18 to 60 need 7 or more hours of sleep a day, adults aged 61 to 64 need 7 to 9 hours, and adults aged 65 and older need 7 to 8 hours. Habits that can improve sleep include going to bed and getting up at the same time every day, keeping the bedroom quiet, relaxing and cool, turning off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime, avoiding large meals and alcohol before bedtime, avoiding caffeine in the afternoon or evening, and exercising regularly. People who regularly have problems sleeping should talk to a healthcare provider, who may ask them to keep a sleep diary.

Source: CDC, About Sleep, published 2024-05-15; ShunShi reviewed 2026-10-31.
Link: https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html

## Physical activity

### Weekly activity for adults

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends that adults aged 18 to 64 do at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity a week, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, or an equivalent combination, plus muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week. People with chronic conditions or disabilities should check with a health professional about the right type and amount of activity.

Source: WHO, Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour (2020) and Physical activity fact sheet; ShunShi reviewed 2026-10-31.
Link: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity

## Healthy eating

### Fruit, vegetables, salt and sugar

The WHO recommends that adults eat at least 400 g (five portions) of fruit and vegetables a day, not counting starchy roots such as potatoes and cassava, limit salt to less than 5 g a day (about one teaspoon), and keep free sugars below 10 percent of total energy intake (about 50 g for a 2,000-calorie diet), with below 5 percent giving additional benefit. Individual needs depend on age, sex, activity and health conditions, so a clinician or registered dietitian should tailor advice for medical conditions.

Source: WHO, Healthy diet fact sheet; ShunShi reviewed 2026-10-31.
Link: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet

## Seasonal mood

### Low mood in darker months

The UK National Health Service (NHS) advises that people with seasonal low mood try to get outside in natural daylight every day, especially around midday, exercise regularly (ideally outdoors), keep up usual activities, stay in touch with friends and family, keep regular sleep and waking times, and avoid excess alcohol and drugs. People who think they have seasonal affective disorder, or whose mood is affecting daily life, should see a GP or other health professional. If you are thinking about harming yourself, contact a local crisis service or emergency number right away.

Source: NHS, Seasonal affective disorder (SAD); ShunShi reviewed 2026-10-31.
Link: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/seasonal-affective-disorder-sad/
