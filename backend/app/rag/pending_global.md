## Stress and mood

### Grounding when feelings are intense

The World Health Organization's illustrated guide "Doing What Matters in Times of Stress" (2020) describes a grounding exercise for emotional storms: notice how you are feeling and what you are thinking; slow down and connect with your body by slowly pressing your feet into the floor, stretching, and slowing your breathing by emptying your lungs and letting them refill slowly; then refocus on the world around you by noticing what you can see, hear, smell and touch. The guide says the exercise takes about 30 seconds and can be done anywhere, and that it does not make difficult feelings disappear.

Source: World Health Organization, Doing What Matters in Times of Stress: An Illustrated Guide, 2020; wording checked 2026-11-01; status: pending review.
Link: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927

### Everyday ways to ease stress

The NHS "10 stress busters" page suggests being active (exercise will not make stress disappear, but it can reduce some of the emotional intensity), taking control of what you can, connecting with people, setting aside some time for yourself, challenging yourself, and helping others. It advises not relying on alcohol, smoking or caffeine to cope, because they may give temporary relief but create new problems, and says it is best to tackle the cause of the stress.

Source: NHS (UK), 10 stress busters; wording checked 2026-11-01; status: pending review.
Link: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/tips-to-reduce-stress/

## Physical activity

### Being active for mental wellbeing

NHS Every Mind Matters says regular physical activity can boost mood, reduce tension, anxiety and stress, and help with sleep, and that any amount of activity is good. It says adults should aim for about 150 minutes of moderate activity each week, including something for muscle strength, and suggests choosing activities you enjoy and moving at your own pace. People with a health condition who are unsure what is safe should ask a healthcare professional.

Source: NHS Every Mind Matters, Be active for your mental health; wording checked 2026-11-01; status: pending review.
Link: https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/be-active-for-your-mental-health/
